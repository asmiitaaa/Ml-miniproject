Adaptive Traffic Signal Control with Deep Reinforcement Learning

A DQN agent controls the signal at a real signalized junction and is compared against the two controllers used in practice: fixed-time (the deployed standard at most Indian junctions) and actuated (sensor-based green extension). Identical traffic, identical random seeds — the controller is the only variable.

Honest reading: the safe DQN controller decisively outperforms fixed-time control — the standard at most Indian junctions — on every metric, and approaches but does not yet beat a well-tuned actuated controller (it trails by ~12–15% on waiting time). An earlier checkpoint appeared to beat actuated control, but it achieved this with a clearance interval shorter than the junction permits, causing simulated collisions; those results were discarded and the controller retrained under a zero-collision safety gate.

Method

  • · Real corridor (17th Cross Rd, HSR Layout) from OpenStreetMap, simulated in SUMO 1.28 with the sublane model (0.4 m) for two-wheeler filtering
  • · Vehicle mix: 45% motorcycle, 15% auto, 28% car, 5% bus, 7% bicycle
  • · DQN (Stable-Baselines3): state = per-approach queues, halting counts, waiting times, phase; action = hold / switch; reward = −Σ waiting time
  • · Safety constraints enforced, not learned: 12 s minimum green, 6 s yellow matching the network
  • · 5 seeds × 2 scenarios × 3 controllers on identical demand; seed-paired comparison

Limitations

  • · Results are for the trained junction; corridor-wide multi-intersection control is future work
  • · Demand is synthetic and calibrated visually, not from field counts
  • · Simulation evidence only — not a field deployment
  • · Actuated control remains the stronger baseline; closing that gap (reward shaping, longer training) is ongoing
